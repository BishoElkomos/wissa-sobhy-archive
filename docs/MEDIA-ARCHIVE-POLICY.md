# Media Archive Policy

## Purpose
The archive treats photographs, video, audio, scanned documents, screenshots, newspaper imagery, public social-media posts and other audiovisual material as historical evidence when provenance and context can be established.

## Media classes
- **Photograph** — original, archival, press, family, church, or reproduced photograph.
- **Video** — interview, news report, ceremony, liturgy, documentary, testimony, or event footage.
- **Audio** — interview, sermon, testimony, radio, oral history, or other recording.
- **Document scan** — certificate, letter, newspaper clipping, program, invitation, manuscript, or official document.
- **Screenshot** — captured web/social-media evidence, retained with URL and capture date.
- **Gallery/collection** — a group of related media items belonging to one source or event.

## Required metadata
Each media item should have, when available:
- stable media ID;
- media type;
- title/caption in Arabic and English;
- date created and/or date published;
- event;
- place;
- people depicted or heard, only when reliably identified;
- original creator/photographer/recordist;
- original publisher/owner;
- source URL;
- archived URL;
- source record ID;
- provenance notes;
- rights/licensing status;
- permitted use;
- technical information when available;
- transcription/description for audio/video;
- language;
- evidence grade;
- relationship to other copies.

## Provenance and duplication
Never discard a useful duplicate. Link copies as:
- original;
- mirror;
- republication;
- cropped/edited derivative;
- screenshot;
- archive capture;
- unknown derivative.

If the same photograph appears on a newspaper page, Facebook post and forum archive, retain all source traces and designate the most authoritative discoverable copy as canonical.

## Identification discipline
Do not identify people in historical images merely because a caption or comment claims an identity. Use contextual evidence, corroborating photographs, event records, family records, or other reliable evidence. Mark uncertain identifications explicitly.

## Rights
The archive should distinguish:
- public-domain material;
- family-owned material;
- material used with permission;
- licensed material;
- embedded third-party material;
- copyrighted material retained as a reference record only.

A source being publicly accessible does not automatically grant republication rights.

## Preservation
For important media, preserve:
- original source URL;
- capture date;
- source page title;
- original filename when available;
- archival copy reference when legally and technically appropriate;
- checksum for locally preserved files in future ingestion pipelines.

Do not alter an original archival image. Derivatives for web presentation must be stored separately and identified as derivatives.

## Audio/video transcription
Where technically feasible, transcripts should record:
- language;
- speaker identification confidence;
- approximate timestamps;
- verbatim transcript where appropriate;
- editorially reviewed summary;
- translation linked to the original transcript.

Do not present machine transcription as a verified quotation without human review.

## Social media
Public Facebook, YouTube and other social-media material can be historically valuable. Capture the page/account name, post date, URL, caption, media, comments that contain substantive historical evidence, and access/capture date. Distinguish the account owner's testimony from reposted material.

## Publishing rule
Media displayed on the public website must have a clear provenance label and rights status. When rights are uncertain, prefer linking or embedding the original source rather than republishing the file.
