# Data Governance & Archival Integrity

## Principle
The archive is a living historical record. New evidence may refine, contradict or correct earlier entries. Corrections must improve the record without erasing its evidentiary history.

## Canonical records
Each person, event, place, organization, source and media item receives a stable identifier. Localized names and content attach to the same identifier.

## Evidence states
- **verified** — supported by a sufficiently authoritative source or converging independent evidence;
- **corroborated** — supported by multiple substantially independent sources;
- **reported** — documented by a source but not independently established;
- **family testimony** — supplied by the family/archive custodian and clearly labeled;
- **oral history** — attributed testimony with provenance;
- **research lead** — useful clue awaiting verification;
- **disputed** — materially conflicting evidence exists;
- **excluded** — demonstrated mismatch, fabrication, or irrelevant identity.

## Revision history
Historical records should retain an audit trail of meaningful corrections. When a date, name, role, identification or interpretation changes, record:
- previous value;
- new value;
- reason;
- evidence used;
- review date;
- reviewer/editor where appropriate.

## Conflict handling
When sources disagree, do not silently select the preferred version. Preserve the competing claims, rank the evidence, explain the basis for the editorial conclusion, and keep the unresolved issue visible where appropriate.

## Identity disambiguation
Names may overlap. Before linking a person to an event, compare available combinations of name, title, location, chronology, institution, family relationship and source context. A name match alone is insufficient for sensitive or consequential assertions.

## Family archive
Family-provided photographs, certificates, letters, memories and dates are valuable primary evidence for family history. They must be labeled by provenance and should not be represented as independently published institutional records unless another source establishes that status.

## Public/private boundary
Only information appropriate for public historical publication should be exposed. Sensitive personal information about living people should not be collected or published merely because it can be found online.

## Source preservation
Every source record should retain its original citation, publication date, URL and archival reference where available. If a source disappears, the archive should preserve enough metadata to identify what was consulted without fabricating a replacement.

## Editorial independence
The archive may have a clear ecclesiastical and cultural context while remaining explicit about whether a statement is:
- an official church record;
- a contemporaneous press report;
- a family account;
- a witness account;
- an archival inference;
- an editorial synthesis.

## Publication gate
No important historical assertion should be promoted to verified status without an evidence review. Search-engine snippets, unsourced social-media comments and copied secondary pages are leads, not automatic verification.
