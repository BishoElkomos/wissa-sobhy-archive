# Archival Citation & Research Guide

## Purpose
Define how visitors, researchers, journalists and future editors should cite material from the Wissa Sobhy Archive.

## Citation hierarchy
Where possible, cite the original historical source first, then the archive record that catalogs or contextualizes it.

Preferred order:
1. Primary document or contemporaneous source.
2. Official ecclesiastical/institutional record.
3. Reliable contemporaneous press report.
4. Independent corroborating source.
5. Scholarly or reputable secondary source.
6. Archival mirror/republication.
7. Family testimony or oral history, clearly attributed.
8. Search-engine result or unsourced social comment only as a research lead.

## Archive citation
Each public record should expose a stable archive identifier where available. A recommended citation form is:

> Wissa Sobhy Archive, “Record Title,” record ID, publication/update date, source(s), accessed date.

## News article citation
Record:
- author if known;
- article title;
- publication;
- publication date;
- event date if different;
- original URL;
- archive URL if available;
- page/section information where applicable.

## Photograph citation
Record:
- photographer/creator if known;
- caption/title;
- date;
- event/place;
- original publisher or collection;
- source URL;
- rights status;
- archive record ID.

## Video/audio citation
Record:
- title;
- creator/publisher;
- platform or broadcaster;
- publication date;
- event/recording date when known;
- timestamp for a quoted passage;
- source URL;
- archive record ID.

## Document citation
Record:
- document type;
- issuing institution/author;
- date;
- title or identifying description;
- collection/provenance;
- page/folio if applicable;
- archive record ID.

## Quotation rules
Quotes must remain faithful to the source. Do not silently modernize, translate or correct wording inside quotation marks. Editorial corrections should be identified outside the quotation or with an explicit editorial note.

## Translation
For significant quotations:
- preserve the original language;
- provide a reviewed translation;
- identify the translation as an archive translation unless an authoritative published translation exists.

## Social media citation
For a public social-media post, cite the account/page, post date, platform, post URL, media description and access date. Distinguish the account owner's original statement from reposted content.

## Missing or unstable links
If the original source is no longer accessible, cite the known original publication details and an archival reference when available. Do not substitute an unrelated page simply because it contains similar wording.

## Research use
Researchers may quote limited portions of source material in accordance with applicable law and the rights attached to the source. The archive does not grant rights it does not own.

## Machine assistance
AI-generated transcription, translation or metadata is a processing aid, not an authoritative historical source. Significant quotations and identifications require editorial review before publication.
