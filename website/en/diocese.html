History of the Diocese of Deir Mawas and Delga

From the historical roots and the unified see to the foundation of the diocese and its modern leadership

Historical roots and the old affiliation

Delga and Deir Mawas in a wider historical setting

This section is presented as a contextual layer of the archive, explaining the historical and ecclesial background within which the modern diocese emerged.

Delga through the ages

  • The roots of the Delga area reach back to ancient times. It is mentioned in major works of geography and history, including the writings of al-Jabarti, Ibn Mammati, Yaqut al-Hamawi, Ibn Duqmaq, al-Jay'aan and al-Suyuti.
  • Delga acquired a distinguished standing as one of the spiritually and culturally weighty towns of Middle Egypt across the centuries.

The old ecclesiastical affiliation

Deir Mawas and Delga were not an independent diocese at first. Historically they belonged to a wider geographic and pastoral entity known as the See of Deirout, Sanabu and Qosqam, before that see was reorganized and divided into independent dioceses.

The 19th and 20th centuries

The bishops of the unified see

According to the historical material supplied to the archive, a number of bishops successively shepherded the region before the modern division.

c. 1835 – c. 1874

Anba Gabriel

Served as bishop of the See of Qosqam and Sanabu until his repose around 1874.

1875 – 1879

Anba Saweros I

Ordained bishop of the See of Sanabu and Qosqam in 1875 by Pope Cyril V, and served until his repose in 1879.

1879 – 1900

Anba Athanasius

Consecrated bishop of the See of Sanabu and Qosqam in 1879, later elevated to metropolitan, and shepherded the see until his repose in 1900.

1901 – June 1925

Anba Saweros II

Ordained bishop of the See of Deirout, Sanabu and Qosqam in 1901; he moved the episcopal seat to the city of Deirout and served until his repose in June 1925.

July 1929 – April 1964

Anba Agabius I

Ordained bishop of Deirout and Sanabu in July 1929 and shepherded the see until his repose in April 1964.

1965 – 8 July 1984

Anba Agabius II

Ordained bishop of the See of Deirout, Sanabu and Qosqam in 1965 and shepherded it until his repose on 8 July 1984.

1984 – 1986

The division of the see and the founding of the independent diocese

The need for division

After the repose of Anba Agabius II in 1984, and with the geographic spread and growing Coptic population, the need emerged to organize pastoral care closer to local communities.

According to the supplied historical account, this came within Pope Shenouda III's vision of reorganizing the large dioceses to provide better care.

The dioceses that emerged from the division

  • The Diocese of Deirout and Sanabu.
  • The Diocese of Qussia and Meir.
  • The Diocese of Deir Mawas and Delga.
In this account, 1986 marks the founding of the diocese as an independent entity, while the 1988 crisis and the ordination of Anba Agabius mark the actual launch of its modern history.
Modern leadership

Metropolitan Agabius and Bishop Boktor

13 November 1988 – 4 February 2025

The late Metropolitan Agabius

Ordained bishop of the Diocese of Deir Mawas and Delga on 13 November 1988 and elevated to metropolitan on 20 November 2022. His era became bound up with the diocese's contemporary history, its services and its churches over more than three decades, until he departed in the Lord on 4 February 2025.

Local Christian heritage

Historic churches and sites

The Monastery of the Virgin Mary and Anba Abram at Delga

  • The monastery's principal historic church is among the oldest churches; according to the supplied material its origins go back to the fourth century AD, around 400.
  • It comprises three sanctuaries and four altars.
  • It holds an engraved stone with Coptic drawings of two gazelles dating to the fourth century.
  • The monastery includes what is known as the "Weeping Column", an ancient baptistery carved from stone, and a historic well.

The Church of the Great Martyr St George at Deir Mawas

  • Excavations of the church's foundations uncovered a baptistery and a water channel, indicating — according to the supplied material — the antiquity of the site as a church or monastery.
  • The evidence mentioned suggests the current historic church was built around 1589 for the martyrs.
  • The historic Church of the Virgin Mary was then built above it in 1840 to accommodate the growing congregation.
1988 – 1989

The founding crisis and the contemporary launch

September 1988

The Qasr Hashmet crisis

The young diocese faced a violent crisis known as the "Qasr Hashmet crisis". The palace was a historic building whose ownership had passed to the Church for use as the diocesan headquarters; sectarian tensions erupted, leading to security forces intervening, the building being closed, and tear gas being used.

4 November 1988

Father Wissa Anba Bishoy chosen for the episcopate

According to the supplied material, on Friday 4 November 1988 Pope Shenouda III approved the request of the notables of Deir Mawas and chose Hegumen Wissa Anba Bishoy as their bishop, in an attempt to contain the crisis and repair the national fabric.

13 November 1988

Episcopal ordination under the name Anba Agabius

The ordination took place under the name "Anba Agabius", continuing the name of the see's earlier bishops, according to the historical account contained in the material.

5 August 1989

The enthronement

Because of the security circumstances linked to the Qasr Hashmet crisis, the enthronement was postponed for several months, then held formally on Saturday 5 August 1989 amid celebrations and the people's joy.

Building and development

The era of construction and institutional service

Renewing and building churches

Metropolitan Agabius led a wide campaign of building and renewal, including the demolition and rebuilding of dilapidated churches — among the examples mentioned are the Church of the Virgin Mary in Deir Mawas, St George Church in El-Rahmaniya and Anba Glinikos Church in Tukh — alongside the founding of new churches.

Church documentation offices

Ten offices were established for documenting church papers and personal-status matters, beginning in 1991.

The consecration house for deaconesses

A dedicated house of consecration for deaconesses was established at the Monastery of the Virgin Mary and Anba Abram in Delga. The first class was accepted in July 2018, and it opened formally on 20 July 2018 with the acceptance of six deaconesses, according to the supplied material.

The services building

A six-floor integrated services building beside the diocesan headquarters, housing the "Good Group" theater, a lending library, halls and a space dedicated to manuscripts.

The Agapi farm

A development project launched on 30 acres along the Western Desert Road, with 18,000 olive trees and animal-production projects, supported by a 60-kilowatt solar power station added in 2025.

Legal defense of church property

A lawsuit was filed in 1994 against the then Minister of Interior claiming 20 million pounds in compensation for the security failure in protecting Qasr Hashmet, according to the historical material supplied to the archive.

Land dispute incident

The archive record carries a real-estate dispute and the detention of priests, with Father Wissa's degree of involvement kept as classified in the evidence record pending further verification.

The Kamilia Shehata crisis

Father Wissa appears in media coverage in an official representative capacity, according to the current record.

The Deir Mawas attacks

The area witnessed violent events with damage to churches and property, and the record connects Father Wissa's role as witness and documenter.

2018

The consecration house opened with its first six deaconesses, according to the supplied material.

2025

Transition of episcopal leadership

Metropolitan Agabius departed in the Lord on 4 February 2025, and Bishop Boktor was ordained bishop of the diocese on 1 June 2025, beginning the current phase of leadership.
