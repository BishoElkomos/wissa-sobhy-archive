Historical Figure

Metropolitan Agabius

Metropolitan of Deir Mawas and Delga • 1944–2025

Identity note: Metropolitan Agabius is not Father Wissa Sobhy Tadros. Before his episcopate, Agabius was known as the monk Father Wissa Anba Bishoy.

Early life and education

Agabius was born on April 2, 1944 in Zagazig, Egypt. He earned a Bachelor of Agriculture from Cairo University in 1968 and worked as an agricultural engineer and later as a supply inspector in Tanta.

Monastic life and priesthood

He entered monastic life at the Monastery of Saint Bishoy in Wadi El Natrun on December 15, 1977. He was ordained a priest on July 17, 1979 and received the rank of hegumen on January 1, 1981.

Service before the episcopate

He served in Tanta and was later delegated by Pope Shenouda III to churches in Cairo beginning in 1985. These years preceded his call to episcopal service.

Episcopate in Deir Mawas and Delga

He was ordained Bishop of Deir Mawas and Delga on November 13, 1988, and enthroned on August 5, 1989. He served the diocese for more than three decades.

Metropolitan rank

He was elevated to metropolitan rank by Pope Tawadros II on November 20, 2022. The official Coptic Orthodox Church record confirms the dates of his birth, monasticism, episcopate, metropolitan rank and repose.

Relationship with Father Wissa Sobhy

Agabius participated in Father Wissa Sobhy's priestly ordination in 1991 and therefore occupies a central place in the archive's documented account of Father Wissa's ministry. The two biographies are kept separate to prevent a historical name similarity from becoming an identity confusion.

Repose and diocesan transition

Metropolitan Agabius departed in the Lord on February 4, 2025, at nearly 81 years of age. The Coptic Orthodox Church reported that he had spent more than 47 years in monastic life, including 36 years as a bishop. Funeral prayers were led by Pope Tawadros II at Saint Mark's Cathedral in Abbassia on February 5, 2025. On June 1, 2025, Bishop Boktor was ordained Bishop of Deir Mawas and Delga, marking the beginning of a new episcopal phase for the diocese.
