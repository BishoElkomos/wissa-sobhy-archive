Delga and Deir Mawas through the ages

The Delga area's roots go back to ancient times; it was mentioned in the writings of Al-Jabarti, Ibn Mammati, Yaqut al-Hamawi, Ibn Duqmaq and Al-Suyuti, and it acquired a distinguished spiritual and civilizational standing in Middle Egypt.

Deir Mawas and Delga were not at first an independent diocese; they were historically tied to a wider pastoral entity known as the see of Deirout, Sanabu and Qusqam, before it was reorganized and divided into independent dioceses.

Founding and the modern launch

In 1986 the old see was divided into three dioceses: Deirout and Sanabu, El-Qusiya and Meir, and Deir Mawas and Delga. In September 1988 the young diocese faced the Kasr Heshmat crisis, and Pope Shenouda III chose Hegumen Wissa Anba Bishoy as its bishop; he was ordained under the name Bishop Agabius on 13 November 1988 and enthroned on 5 August 1989.

Years of building and institutional service

Renovating and building churches; founding the church documentation offices in 1991; the house of consecration and the consecrated virgins at the Monastery of the Virgin Mary and St Abram at Delga (opened 12 February 2018); the services building next to the Metropolis; and the Agapy Farm development project.
